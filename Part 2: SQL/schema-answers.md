## Design choices

The schema is organized around four straightforward relationships. Guests are the central record — one row per person, identified by their channel handle (a phone number, email address, or platform ID) and the channel type they use to communicate. Reservations point directly to a guest via a foreign key, keeping the link between a stay and the person who booked it simple and queryable with a single join. Conversations sit between guests and reservations, acting as the thread that ties a guest's messages to the context of their visit. Messages then hang off conversations, and every message — regardless of whether it came in via WhatsApp, email, or any other channel — lives in the same table, making cross-channel reporting a plain `WHERE` filter rather than a union across multiple tables.

Plain integer primary keys were chosen over UUIDs throughout. They are easier to read in query results, simpler to use in test data, and require no extensions. The `ai_query_type` and `ai_confidence` columns live directly on the `messages` table rather than in a separate analysis table because they are always needed alongside the message itself — a dedicated table would just mean a mandatory join on every fetch. The `reservation_id` on conversations is nullable by design, because guests often reach out before a reservation is confirmed, and forcing a reservation link would make those early conversations impossible to store cleanly.

## Hardest decision

The hardest call was putting `channel_type` and `channel_handle` directly on the `guests` table rather than in a separate table. The intuitive alternative is a dedicated channels table where one guest can have multiple addresses — a WhatsApp number, an email, a Booking.com ID — all linked by a foreign key. That is more flexible, but it immediately raises questions that would complicate the schema considerably: which channel is canonical, how do you prevent duplicate guests from forming when the same person contacts you via two channels, and how do you query the most recent contact method without a subquery. Keeping channel information flat on the guest row makes all of those questions disappear. The `UNIQUE` constraint on `(channel_type, channel_handle)` ensures no two guest records can claim the same address on the same platform, which is the most important integrity guarantee. The tradeoff is that a guest who uses two channels would create two rows rather than one, but that is a complexity worth deferring until the data model is ready for it.5
